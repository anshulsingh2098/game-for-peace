- **Position, footprint, height, Wikidata id and opening hours come from OpenStreetMap** (found by the Wikidata id or the name within a few hundred metres of the place). `src: "osm"` means the feature was found; `src: "list"` means it was not and the position written in the list is used with a small square. Heights are real only where OSM has a `height` tag (or at least 3 `building:levels`) or where the list gives one (a figure from public sources); otherwise null.
- **Cards** are one paragraph of facts, written from public encyclopaedic knowledge (not copied text). Dates were cross-checked against the Wikidata inception date where Wikidata has one (a few differ and the card says why, for example Bangla Sahib: 1664 for the Guru's visit, 1783 for the present building). They are not checked against a second printed source: treat them as a first draft for a person to review.
- **`look` notes** (materials and colours) are written from general knowledge of how each place looks. **They were not checked against photographs in this session** (no images were looked at or downloaded); the model-building package (D8) should compare them with Wikimedia Commons categories, looking only and copying no files.

- Drive times use the pack's street graph (one-ways respected, footways excluded) at 55 % of each road class's speed, a guess for ordinary urban traffic, not measured; peak hours are slower. Walk times use 4.8 km/h along any street except motorways and trunk roads. A leg longer than 4 km on foot shows a dash.

### Cards and look notes

**India Gate** (इंडिया गेट, `india-gate`, 1931): A 42 m war memorial arch designed by Edwin Lutyens. The foundation stone was laid in 1921 and it was dedicated in 1931. It carries the names of more than 13,000 soldiers of the British Indian Army who died in the First World War and the Third Anglo-Afghan War. The eternal flame that stood beneath it was merged into the nearby National War Memorial in January 2022.  
*Look:* tall arch of red and buff sandstone on a low stepped base, plain, with a shallow canopy to its east; lawns and tree-lined avenues around it

**Kartavya Path (Rajpath)** (कर्तव्य पथ, `kartavya-path`, 1931): The ceremonial avenue of about 3 km that runs from Rashtrapati Bhavan through Vijay Chowk to India Gate. Called Kingsway and then Rajpath, it was renamed Kartavya Path in September 2022 after a redevelopment under the Central Vista project. The Republic Day parade is held here every 26 January.  
*Look:* two long canals with lawns and rows of trees on both sides, wide sandstone-red paths, Rashtrapati Bhavan at the west end and India Gate at the east end

**Rashtrapati Bhavan** (राष्ट्रपति भवन, `rashtrapati-bhavan`, 1929): The official residence of the President of India, designed by Edwin Lutyens and completed in 1929 as the Viceroy's House. It has 340 rooms and a central dome that rises over a long red and cream sandstone front. The Jaipur Column stands in the forecourt, and the formal gardens behind (Amrit Udyan since 2023) open to visitors in season.  
*Look:* very long cream and red sandstone building on a raised plinth, a central copper-green dome, rows of columns with chhajjas, the Jaipur Column in front, formal gardens behind

**Sansad Bhavan (new Parliament House)** (नया संसद भवन, `sansad-bhavan-new`, 2023): The new Parliament building, triangular in plan, was inaugurated on 28 May 2023 beside the old one. The Lok Sabha chamber seats 888 members and the Rajya Sabha chamber 384. It is part of the Central Vista redevelopment; construction began in December 2020.  
*Look:* triangular building of cream and red sandstone with carved jaali screens, a national emblem on the roof, open courtyards inside

**Samvidhan Sadan (old Parliament House)** (संविधान सदन (पुराना संसद भवन), `samvidhan-sadan`, 1927): The circular colonnaded building designed by Herbert Baker and Edwin Lutyens, built between 1921 and 1927 and in use as Parliament until 2023. It is about 170 m across and ringed by 144 columns. It was renamed Samvidhan Sadan (House of the Constitution) when the new building opened.  
*Look:* a circular colonnaded building of buff sandstone with a ring of tall columns and a low central dome, set in lawns inside a wall

**North Block** (नॉर्थ ब्लॉक, `north-block`, 1927): One of the two Secretariat buildings flanking Raisina Hill, designed by Herbert Baker and built in the 1920s, finished by about 1930. It has long housed the Ministries of Finance and Home Affairs. Its twin, South Block, stands opposite across the road.  
*Look:* long red and cream sandstone secretariat building with domed pavilions, chhatris and deep arcades facing Raisina Hill

**South Block** (साउथ ब्लॉक, `south-block`, 1927): The southern Secretariat building on Raisina Hill, the twin of North Block and also designed by Herbert Baker, built in the 1920s and finished by about 1930. It has long housed the Ministries of Defence and External Affairs.  
*Look:* red and cream sandstone mirror image of North Block with domed pavilions and arcades

**National War Memorial** (राष्ट्रीय समर स्मारक, `national-war-memorial`, 2019): Inaugurated on 25 February 2019 behind India Gate, it honours the soldiers of independent India who died in war and operations. The names of 25,942 soldiers are inscribed on granite walls arranged in four concentric circles called Amar Chakra, Veerta Chakra, Tyag Chakra and Rakshak Chakra.  
*Look:* four concentric circles of dark granite walls and an obelisk, set in lawns behind India Gate

**National Museum** (राष्ट्रीय संग्रहालय, `national-museum`, 1960): India's largest museum, on Janpath, with more than 200,000 works spanning some 5,000 years, including Harappan objects, miniature paintings and textiles. The museum opened in 1949 in Rashtrapati Bhavan and moved to its own building in 1960.  
*Look:* large circular-court building in pale stone with a central dome, on the corner of Janpath and Maulana Azad Road

**Teen Murti Bhavan** (तीन मूर्ति भवन, `teen-murti-bhavan`, 1930): Built around 1930 as the residence of the British Commander-in-Chief, it became the home of Jawaharlal Nehru until 1964 and is now the Prime Ministers' Museum. It takes its name from the Teen Murti (three soldiers) memorial at the gate.  
*Look:* large cream-coloured colonial residence with a columned portico and wide lawns, a monument of three soldier statues at the gate

**Supreme Court of India** (भारत का उच्चतम न्यायालय, `supreme-court`, 1958): The apex court of India, in a building inaugurated in 1958 on Tilak Marg. Its plan is said to resemble the scales of justice, with a central block under a dome and two wings.  
*Look:* wide pale building with two long wings, a central pillared block and a dome, set in lawns near Pragati Maidan

**Connaught Place (Rajiv Chowk)** (कनॉट प्लेस, `connaught-place`, 1933): Delhi's central business and shopping district, laid out by Robert Tor Russell and built between 1929 and 1933, named after the Duke of Connaught. Two concentric circles of colonnaded white buildings are joined by radial roads, and the central park sits over the underground Palika Bazaar. Officially renamed Rajiv Chowk, it is still called Connaught Place by almost everyone.  
*Look:* two concentric rings of white stucco colonnaded buildings in a Georgian style, three storeys with arcades, a central park above the underground Palika Bazaar, bright shop hoardings above the arcades

**Jantar Mantar** (जंतर मंतर, `jantar-mantar`, 1724): An astronomical observatory built in 1724 by Maharaja Jai Singh II of Jaipur. Its giant masonry instruments measure time and the positions of the sun, moon and stars; the Samrat Yantra, a triangular sundial, is about 21 m high.  
*Look:* large geometric masonry instruments painted red-ochre and white: a huge triangular stair-gnomon, two bowl-shaped structures, circular walls with slits

**Agrasen ki Baoli** (अग्रसेन की बावली, `agrasen-ki-baoli`, 1400): A protected stepwell near Connaught Place, about 60 m long and 15 m wide, with 103 steps down to the water level. Tradition says it was first built by the legendary king Agrasen; the present structure dates to about the 14th century.  
*Look:* a long narrow stepwell of grey and buff stone with arched niches on three levels, steep stairs descending to a dry well, hidden between modern office blocks

**Gurudwara Bangla Sahib** (गुरुद्वारा बंगला साहिब, `bangla-sahib`, 1783): The Sikh house of worship associated with the eighth Guru, Har Krishan, who stayed here in 1664. The present building was raised in 1783. It has a gold dome, a sacred pool (sarovar) and a free community kitchen (langar) that serves thousands every day.  
*Look:* white marble building with a shining gold onion dome and smaller gold chhatris, a flagpole (Nishan Sahib), a large square sarovar (tank) beside it

**Laxminarayan (Birla) Mandir** (लक्ष्मीनारायण मंदिर (बिड़ला मंदिर), `birla-mandir`, 1939): A Hindu temple dedicated to Lakshmi and Narayan on Mandir Marg, built by the industrialist B. D. Birla and opened by Mahatma Gandhi in 1939. It is built in the northern Nagara style with several shikhara spires.  
*Look:* multi-tiered temple of red sandstone and cream marble with tall curved shikhara spires and carved panels, gardens around it

**Safdarjung's Tomb** (सफ़दरजंग का मक़बरा, `safdarjung-tomb`, 1754): A garden tomb of sandstone and marble built in 1754 for Safdarjung, the Nawab of Awadh. It is among the last great garden tombs of the Mughal tradition, set at the centre of a walled four-part garden.  
*Look:* red and buff sandstone mausoleum with a white marble dome on a high platform at the centre of a walled char-bagh garden, canals and fountains

**Lodhi Garden** (लोधी उद्यान, `lodhi-garden`, 1936): A park of about 90 acres holding 15th and 16th century tombs of the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties, including the Bara Gumbad, the Shish Gumbad and the tombs of Muhammad Shah and Sikandar Lodi. It was landscaped in 1936 as Lady Willingdon Park and is now Delhi's favourite place for a morning walk.  
*Look:* large park with old grey-stone domed tombs among lawns, big shade trees, a bridge over a pond, joggers' paths

**Hauz Khas Fort and Village** (हौज़ खास, `hauz-khas`, 1352): A medieval complex around a large reservoir first dug by Alauddin Khalji in the early 14th century. Firoz Shah Tughlaq built a madrasa and later his own tomb on its bank (he died in 1388). The village beside it is now a lane of galleries, boutiques and cafes.  
*Look:* grey stone ruins of a madrasa and domed tomb above a large lake, with trees, and a lane of boutique cafes in the village

**Purana Qila** (पुराना क़िला, `purana-qila`, 1540): The Old Fort, begun by the Mughal emperor Humayun around 1533 and finished under his rival Sher Shah Suri. Its walls run about 1.5 km and are around 18 m high with three gates. Inside are the Qila-i-Kuhna mosque (1541) and the Sher Mandal tower.  
*Look:* tall rough red and grey sandstone walls with three big gates and bastions, a mosque (Qila-i-Kuhna) and an octagonal tower (Sher Mandal) inside lawns

**National Zoological Park** (राष्ट्रीय प्राणी उद्यान, `delhi-zoo`, 1959): Delhi's zoo, opened in 1959 on about 176 acres beside Purana Qila. It is known for its tigers, rhinoceroses and a large collection of birds in wooded grounds.  
*Look:* wooded grounds with ponds and open enclosures, the walls of Purana Qila on its north side

**Humayun's Tomb** (हुमायूँ का मक़बरा, `humayuns-tomb`, 1572): The tomb of the second Mughal emperor Humayun, built between about 1565 and 1572 and designed by the Persian architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas. It is the first great garden tomb of the subcontinent and a model for the Taj Mahal. Red sandstone is inlaid with white marble under a double dome about 47 m high. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1993).  
*Look:* a huge red sandstone mausoleum with white marble borders and a double white-marble dome, on a raised stone platform in a square char-bagh garden with water channels

**Hazrat Nizamuddin Dargah** (हज़रत निज़ामुद्दीन दरगाह, `nizamuddin-dargah`, 1325): The shrine of the Sufi saint Nizamuddin Auliya (died 1325), reached through narrow lanes of flower and food sellers. The poet Amir Khusro is buried nearby. Qawwali is sung here on Thursday evenings.  
*Look:* small white marble shrine with a dome and screens in a crowded lane of flower and chadar sellers

**Sunder Nursery** (सुंदर नर्सरी, `sunder-nursery`, 1930): A restored 90-acre heritage park next to Humayun's Tomb, reopened in 2018 after a project by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture. It holds 16th century monuments such as Sunder Burj and the tomb of Mirza Muzaffar Hussain among lawns and groves.  
*Look:* landscaped park of lawns, lakes and old trees with several 16th century domed tombs in pale stone

**Khan Market** (ख़ान मार्केट, `khan-market`, 1951): A compact arcaded market built in the early 1950s to resettle refugees from the north-west and named after the leader Khan Abdul Jabbar Khan. Today it is known for bookshops, cafes and boutiques.  
*Look:* two-storey arcaded blocks painted cream and white with colourful shop signs, narrow lanes, crowded parking

**Dilli Haat, INA** (दिल्ली हाट, आई.एन.ए., `dilli-haat-ina`, 1994): An open-air craft and food bazaar opened in 1994 by Delhi Tourism. Craftspeople from across India rent stalls on a rotating basis, and the food court serves dishes from many states.  
*Look:* open-air bazaar of brick-red walkways with small thatched-roof or tiled stalls and a food court, carved wooden doors and hanging textiles

**Bharat Mandapam (Pragati Maidan)** (भारत मंडपम, `bharat-mandapam`, 2023): The International Exhibition-Convention Centre built on the Pragati Maidan fairground and inaugurated on 26 July 2023. It hosted the G20 Leaders' Summit in September 2023. The Pragati Maidan grounds have held the annual India International Trade Fair since 1980.  
*Look:* very large modern convention complex with a faceted copper-gold and cream facade, wide plazas, pale stone paving

**Major Dhyan Chand National Stadium** (मेजर ध्यानचंद राष्ट्रीय स्टेडियम, `major-dhyan-chand-stadium`, 1933): A hockey stadium near India Gate, opened in 1933 as the Irwin Amphitheatre and renamed in 2002 after the hockey great Major Dhyan Chand. It was the main stadium of the first Asian Games in 1951.  
*Look:* oval grey stadium with a covered stand and floodlight masts, behind the Princes' Park area near India Gate

**Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium** (जवाहरलाल नेहरू स्टेडियम, `jln-stadium`, 1982): A multi-purpose stadium of about 60,000 seats built for the 1982 Asian Games and rebuilt for the 2010 Commonwealth Games, whose opening and closing ceremonies it hosted. It stages athletics, football and large concerts.  
*Look:* huge oval concrete bowl with a white angular roof structure of steel masts, wide forecourt

**Arun Jaitley Stadium (Feroz Shah Kotla)** (अरुण जेटली स्टेडियम (फ़िरोज़ शाह कोटला), `arun-jaitley-stadium`, 1883): One of India's oldest international cricket grounds, in use since 1883 and a Test venue since 1948. It was renamed in 2019 after the former finance minister Arun Jaitley and is home ground of Delhi's cricket teams.  
*Look:* cricket ground with tiered stands in blue and grey, floodlight towers, beside the old Kotla fort walls

**Feroz Shah Kotla** (फ़िरोज़ शाह कोटला, `firoz-shah-kotla`, 1354): The ruined citadel of Firozabad, built by Sultan Firoz Shah Tughlaq in 1354. On top of a stepped pavilion stands a polished sandstone pillar of the emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE), which Firoz Shah had brought from Haryana in 1356.  
*Look:* ruined rubble-stone walls and a pyramid-like tiered pavilion topped by an ancient sandstone pillar

**Red Fort (Lal Qila)** (लाल क़िला, `red-fort`, 1648): The fort-palace of the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan, built between 1638 and 1648 when he founded Shahjahanabad. Red sandstone walls about 2.4 km long rise from 18 m on the river side to 33 m at the city side. The prime minister raises the flag from its ramparts each 15 August. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2007).  
*Look:* massive red sandstone walls with crenellations and domed octagonal towers, the arched Lahori Gate with white marble pavilions above, marble palaces and lawns inside

**Jama Masjid** (जामा मस्जिद, `jama-masjid`, 1656): One of India's largest mosques, built under Shah Jahan between 1644 and 1656. The courtyard holds about 25,000 people. Three domes of white marble striped with black rise above the prayer hall and two minarets stand about 41 m high. It sits on a small hill opposite the Red Fort and is entered by three gateways.  
*Look:* red sandstone mosque striped with white marble, three white-and-black-striped onion domes, two tall minarets, a vast courtyard reached by wide stairways

**Chandni Chowk** (चाँदनी चौक, `chandni-chowk`, 1650): The main bazaar of Old Delhi, laid out in the 1650s by Shah Jahan's daughter Jahanara Begum, running west from the Red Fort's Lahori Gate to the Fatehpuri Masjid. Side lanes specialise in silver, wedding cloth, spices, books and sweets; the street is shared by pedestrians, cycle rickshaws and handcarts.  
*Look:* a dense bazaar street under a mesh of electric wires, cloth and sweet shops in old arcaded havelis, rickshaws and handcarts, signs in Hindi and Urdu

**Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib** (गुरुद्वारा सीस गंज साहिब, `sis-ganj-sahib`, 1783): A gurdwara on Chandni Chowk that marks the place where the ninth Sikh Guru, Tegh Bahadur, was executed in 1675. The present building dates from 1783.  
*Look:* white marble gurdwara with gilded domes and a flagpole on Chandni Chowk

**Fatehpuri Masjid** (फतेहपुरी मस्जिद, `fatehpuri-masjid`, 1650): A mosque at the western end of Chandni Chowk, built in 1650 by Fatehpuri Begum, one of Shah Jahan's wives. Its single large dome and long prayer hall face a courtyard with a tank, and shops line the old market around it.  
*Look:* red sandstone mosque with one large flattened dome and a tall gateway at the western end of Chandni Chowk

**Khari Baoli (spice market)** (खारी बावली, `khari-baoli`, 1650): A wholesale market of dried fruit, nuts, herbs and spices on the road behind Fatehpuri Masjid, dating to the 17th century and often called Asia's largest wholesale spice market. The air is thick with chilli and cardamom and goods move by porters and handcarts.  
*Look:* narrow street lined with sacks of chillies, turmeric and dried fruit under old balconies, porters carrying loads

**Raj Ghat** (राज घाट, `raj-ghat`, 1948): The memorial to Mahatma Gandhi on the bank of the Yamuna, where he was cremated on 31 January 1948. A simple black marble platform stands in open lawns, visited barefoot. Memorials to other leaders, including Shanti Van and Shakti Sthal, lie close by.  
*Look:* a plain square platform of black marble in the middle of wide green lawns with an eternal flame at one end, trees and walkways

**Old Delhi Railway Station** (पुरानी दिल्ली रेलवे स्टेशन, `old-delhi-station`, 1864): Delhi Junction, the oldest of Delhi's main railway stations, opened in 1864 when the line from Howrah reached the city. Its red sandstone building stands close to the Red Fort and Chandni Chowk.  
*Look:* a long red sandstone Victorian-style station front with towers and arched windows, crowded forecourt

**New Delhi Railway Station** (नई दिल्ली रेलवे स्टेशन, `new-delhi-station`, 1926): The main railway station of the capital, opened in 1926 between Paharganj and Ajmeri Gate and now with 16 platforms. It is the origin or stop of the Rajdhani and Shatabdi express trains.  
*Look:* red brick and sandstone station block with a tall clock tower on the Paharganj side and a modern glass facade on the Ajmeri Gate side

**Maharana Pratap ISBT, Kashmere Gate** (महाराणा प्रताप अंतर्राज्यीय बस अड्डा, कश्मीरी गेट, `kashmere-gate-isbt`, 1976): The main inter-state bus terminal of Delhi, opened in 1976 beside the Kashmere Gate Metro interchange, with buses to Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Jammu and Kashmir.  
*Look:* large concrete bus terminal with long bays under a roof, red and green buses and rows of auto-rickshaws outside

**Anand Vihar ISBT** (आनंद विहार अंतर्राज्यीय बस अड्डा, `anand-vihar-isbt`, date n/a): Delhi's inter-state bus terminal on the Uttar Pradesh border, serving Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand, next to Anand Vihar Railway Terminal and the Metro station.  
*Look:* wide bus terminal with big bays on the eastern border near the Anand Vihar railway terminal and metro

**Sarai Kale Khan ISBT** (सराय काले खां अंतर्राज्यीय बस अड्डा, `sarai-kale-khan-isbt`, date n/a): A major inter-state bus terminal near Hazrat Nizamuddin Railway Station and the Ring Road, with buses to Haryana, Rajasthan and western Uttar Pradesh.  
*Look:* bus terminal beside the Ring Road and the Nizamuddin railway station, rows of buses to Rajasthan, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh

**Hazrat Nizamuddin Railway Station** (हज़रत निज़ामुद्दीन रेलवे स्टेशन, `nizamuddin-station`, date n/a): One of Delhi's four main railway stations, named after the nearby Sufi shrine, with many trains to Mumbai, Goa, Kerala and the south.  
*Look:* long platforms under wide canopies, a plain station building in beige and maroon, near the Nizamuddin dargah

**Anand Vihar Railway Terminal** (आनंद विहार टर्मिनल, `anand-vihar-terminal`, 2009): A railway terminal on the eastern edge of Delhi opened in 2009 to ease crowding at the older stations; it connects to the Anand Vihar ISBT and the Metro.  
*Look:* modern station with long platforms and a glass-fronted concourse beside the bus terminal

**Delhi Sarai Rohilla Railway Station** (दिल्ली सराय रोहिल्ला रेलवे स्टेशन, `sarai-rohilla-station`, 1873): A main railway station in north-west Delhi, opened in 1873, serving trains towards Rajasthan, Punjab and Gujarat.  
*Look:* mid-sized station with platforms and canopies north-west of Old Delhi

**Qutub Minar** (क़ुतुब मीनार, `qutub-minar`, 1220): A 72.5 m victory tower of five tapering storeys, begun in 1193 by Qutb ud-Din Aibak and completed by his successors; the top storeys were rebuilt in 1368 by Firoz Shah Tughlaq. Fluted red sandstone and marble are inscribed with verses from the Quran. The tower rises over the Mehrauli complex that also holds the 7 m Iron Pillar of the 5th century. UNESCO World Heritage Site (1993).  
*Look:* a tall fluted tower of red sandstone and cream marble in five tapering storeys, balconies with carved brackets, bands of Arabic calligraphy

**Mehrauli Archaeological Park** (महरौली पुरातत्व उद्यान, `mehrauli-park`, 1200): An archaeological park of about 200 ha with more than 400 monuments spanning roughly a thousand years, from the Qila Rai Pithora (the first city of Delhi) to Mughal and British period buildings. It includes the Jamali Kamali mosque and tomb, Balban's tomb and Adham Khan's tomb.  
*Look:* overgrown grey-stone tombs, mosques and stepwells among rocky scrub and big trees, narrow paths

**Tughlaqabad Fort** (तुगलकाबाद क़िला, `tughlaqabad-fort`, 1325): A ruined fortress city built from 1321 to 1325 by Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq, with great sloping stone walls. A causeway once crossed a lake to his tomb, which stands just outside.  
*Look:* massive sloping walls of rough grey rubble stone, bastions, ruined palaces on rocky ground, a causeway to the tomb of Ghiyath al-Din

**Lotus Temple** (लोटस टेंपल, `lotus-temple`, 1986): The Bahai House of Worship, completed in 1986 to a design by the Iranian-Canadian architect Fariborz Sahba. Twenty-seven free-standing marble-clad petals form a lotus about 34 m high, with nine entrances opening to a central hall. It is open to people of all faiths and visitors keep silence inside.  
*Look:* a huge white marble lotus of 27 petals in three rings rising from nine blue-green pools, surrounded by green lawns and paths

**ISKCON Temple, East of Kailash** (इस्कॉन मंदिर, `iskcon-delhi`, 1998): The Sri Sri Radha Parthasarathi Mandir at Hare Krishna Hill in East of Kailash, opened in 1998 by the Hare Krishna movement. Evening aarti and a museum of Vedic culture draw large crowds.  
*Look:* large cream and rose-pink temple with stylised spires on a hill, gardens and marble steps

**Swaminarayan Akshardham** (स्वामीनारायण अक्षरधाम, `akshardham`, 2005): A Hindu temple complex opened in November 2005 on the bank of the Yamuna. The central monument is about 43 m tall and built of pink sandstone and white marble, carved with thousands of figures and a plinth of 148 stone elephants. Phones and cameras are not allowed inside.  
*Look:* an enormous pink sandstone and white marble temple covered with carved figures and pillars, a central dome and spires, a wall of stone elephants, wide fountains and lawns

**Garden of Five Senses** (पंच इंद्रिय उद्यान, `garden-of-five-senses`, 2003): A 20-acre park at Said-ul-Ajaib in Mehrauli opened in 2003, built around a rocky site with themed gardens, sculptures and an annual spring flower festival.  
*Look:* landscaped garden of lawns, rocky outcrops, water channels and sculptures with an amphitheatre and flower festival lawns

**National Rail Museum** (राष्ट्रीय रेल संग्रहालय, `national-rail-museum`, 1977): A railway museum in Chanakyapuri opened in 1977, with historic locomotives and coaches in an open-air yard, including the Fairy Queen steam engine of 1855 and royal saloons, plus a toy train ride.  
*Look:* an open-air yard of old steam locomotives and carriages on narrow-gauge track, with a toy train and a museum hall

**Indira Gandhi International Airport, Terminal 3** (इंदिरा गांधी अंतर्राष्ट्रीय हवाई अड्डा, टर्मिनल 3, `igi-t3`, 2010): Delhi's international terminal, opened in July 2010 before the Commonwealth Games, and one of the largest airport terminals in Asia when it opened. Its hall has a row of giant hand gestures (mudras) as sculptures, and the Airport Express metro serves it.  
*Look:* very large glass and steel terminal with a long curved roof, a giant hand-gesture (mudra) sculpture wall in the arrivals hall, elevated approach roads

**Yashobhoomi (India International Convention and Expo Centre)** (यशोभूमि, `yashobhoomi`, 2023): A convention and expo centre in Dwarka Sector 25 inaugurated in September 2023, linked to the airport by the Airport Express line. It is among the largest such venues in India.  
*Look:* very large convention complex with a glass facade and a gold-patterned roof, wide plazas, in Dwarka next to a metro station

**Sarojini Nagar Market** (सरोजिनी नगर मार्केट, `sarojini-nagar-market`, date n/a): A huge open-air clothing market named after the poet Sarojini Naidu, known for cheap fashion, export surplus and tight bargaining. It is packed at weekends.  
*Look:* a crowded open-air street market of tarpaulin-covered stalls piled with clothes, bright price boards, narrow aisles

**Lajpat Nagar Central Market** (लाजपत नगर सेंट्रल मार्केट, `lajpat-nagar-market`, date n/a): A busy market named after the nationalist Lala Lajpat Rai, in a neighbourhood built in the 1950s for refugees. It is known for salwar suits, wedding shopping and chaat.  
*Look:* multi-storey shop fronts with big hoardings over narrow lanes, fabric and jewellery shops, street food stalls

**Karol Bagh (Ajmal Khan Road)** (करोल बाग, `karol-bagh-market`, date n/a): One of Delhi's largest shopping districts, along Ajmal Khan Road and Gaffar Market, known for jewellery, clothing, and electronics. The Karol Bagh Hanuman statue, 108 ft tall, stands at a nearby junction.  
*Look:* dense strip of multi-storey shop fronts with big hoardings above a metro viaduct, jewellery and clothing shops

**Palika Bazaar** (पालिका बाज़ार, `palika-bazaar`, date n/a): An underground, air-conditioned market under the central park of Connaught Place, known for electronics, cheap clothing and bargaining.  
*Look:* a low-ceilinged underground market with rows of small shops and bright signs below the Connaught Place central park

**Janpath Market** (जनपथ मार्केट, `janpath-market`, date n/a): A shopping street south of Connaught Place with handicraft stalls, silver jewellery and textiles. A lane of Tibetan market stalls runs alongside.  
*Look:* rows of street stalls and shops in front of cream colonial buildings, textiles, jewellery and handicrafts, Tibetan market lane

**Majnu ka Tilla (Tibetan Colony)** (मजनू का टीला, `majnu-ka-tilla`, 1960): A Tibetan refugee colony on the Yamuna bank, set up in 1960, known for momos, thukpa, Tibetan handicrafts, prayer flags and a Buddhist temple.  
*Look:* narrow lanes with prayer flags and signs in English and Tibetan, small cafes, a gold-roofed Buddhist temple, near the Yamuna

**Nehru Place** (नेहरू प्लेस, `nehru-place`, 1965): A commercial district planned in the 1960s, now the city's electronics and IT hardware market, with many shops of laptops, phones and printers and their repair stalls.  
*Look:* a grey tower-and-plaza business district of mid-rise office blocks with computer and phone shops, hoardings, crowded steps

## Landmark models: status (package D8, 17 Nov 2026)

Models are built in code (`src/world/city/landmarks/`, no files, no images) from each landmark's own record: the footprint's oriented box (centre, yaw, half extents) and the height come from `landmarks.json`; layouts that the data does not hold (interiors, annexes) are placed along the data's own axes from the known plans and are **approximate**. Each model has two layers (base always, detail within 380 m), collision circles, a name board (English over Hindi) and an info card when you are within 70 m. Look: written from knowledge of the places and the `look` notes; **no photographs were looked at in this session** (no image search was available), so colours and proportions are a first draft for a person who knows the place to judge.
