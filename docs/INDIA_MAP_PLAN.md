# India map plan: an India-only game (drafted 18 Nov 2026)

The owner's direction: the whole game is **India only**, with the real shape of the country, its real road network (National Highways, the Golden Quadrilateral and the two corridors) and its real railways (the trunk routes between the big cities), and the feel of an Indian GTA-style game. This is the plan and what was built first. Nothing here is deployed.

## 1. Sources and licence (read before using any data)
- **City positions** are facts (latitude and longitude, to about 0.1 degree), entered by hand in `src/world/india_network.js`. **Corridor lists** (which cities a highway or trunk railway passes) come from public descriptions: [Golden Quadrilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Quadrilateral), [North-South and East-West Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-South_and_East-West_Corridor), [NH 44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_44_(India)), the Indian Railways Golden Quadrilateral and Golden Diagonal (the six trunk routes: four sides and two diagonals, about 9,100 route km, carrying over half of the traffic).
- **The two reference maps the owner supplied are not used as data.** They are Wikimedia maps under share-alike licences, and tracing them would bind us to those terms. We draw our own geometry from facts.
- **Exact road alignments and rail track** would need OpenStreetMap (ODbL attribution and share-alike) or the government's open data. That is still **the owner's decision** (see `docs/REGIONS.md`). Until it is made, the network is a schematic of corridors between cities, not surveyed roads.
- Border and coast: only a rough coastline sketch is drawn (not an authoritative boundary). For any shipped map the owner should pick the official Survey of India outline.
- Not checked against NHAI or Railways tables: highway numbers (NH44, NH48, NH19, NH16, NH27) and the intermediate cities are from secondary sources that disagree in places. They are labelled approximate in the data.

## 2. What the data module holds (built)
`src/world/india_network.js`: about 90 cities with tier (metro, major, city, town), state and tags (port, airport, hill, pilgrimage); the highways (Golden Quadrilateral as four sides, North-South Corridor, East-West Corridor, plus NH-style links) and the six rail trunk routes, each an ordered list of city ids. Helpers: distances (great circle times a road factor), route length, game length at a scale, travel time, a nearest-city lookup, a shortest road path between any two cities. `india_map.html` draws it. The self-test checks the data (every id known, every position in India, every leg a plausible length, the network connected, every region record near its city).

## 3. Scale (the main design decision)
India is about 3,000 km tall. Driving it 1:1 is not a game. Proposed, as the plan's `metresPerGameMetre` already allows:
- **Cities and towns:** 1:1 playable districts (the Mumbai district is the model), built one at a time.
- **Highways between them:** compressed about **1:20 to 1:30**, so Delhi to Mumbai (about 1,400 road km) is about 50 to 70 km of driving, roughly 35 to 45 minutes at highway speed. Landscape along the way changes by state (Rajasthan desert, Deccan plateau, Western Ghats, Gangetic plain).
- **Rail and fast travel:** trunk railways are rideable (trains as vehicles and as fast travel with a short cutscene) so the player never drives 40 minutes to cross a state twice.
- Open question for the owner: one continuous world (hard, streaming-heavy, N8 sectors help) or highway "legs" loaded between region hubs (simpler, the plan's current "full reload" transition, later "seamless").

## 5. Replacing the non-India worlds
The game still has five old worlds (default hills, Alps, Himalayas, California, southern coast). **India-only means replacing them**, with the terrain recipes kept and new region records: Himachal (Manali and Shimla road), Ladakh (Leh high desert), Rajasthan (Jodhpur and Jaisalmer, desert highway), Kerala (backwaters, palms, Kochi), and a Deccan plateau (Pune to Bengaluru). **Built 18 Nov 2026:** `himachal` (ghat road, deodar and pine, Atal Tunnel), `ladakh` (cold desert, thin road), `rajasthan` (dry ochre plains, six-lane national highway), `kerala` (palms, coast, lighthouse, busy two-lane road) and `deccan` (green hills, six-lane expressway). The five old ids (`default`, `alps`, `himalayas`, `california`, `south`) still load by URL and from old saved choices (which move to the Indian replacement) but are hidden from the picker; the game now starts in Mumbai. The new worlds reuse the old terrain recipes with Indian palettes, plants, roads and traffic; they are impressions, not maps, and none has been judged by a person. Ground dryness is a new optional palette number, `arid`. The old ids stay loadable by URL for old share codes until the owner says otherwise.

## 6. Order of work (proposed)
1. (built) the schematic network, its checks and the 2D map.
2. Five Indian region records re-skinning the old worlds (built if listed in the log).
3. A highway generator: a road between two cities follows the corridor, compressed, through the region terrains in between (needs the scale decision).
4. Toll plazas, km stones, bilingual signs, dhabas along the highways (cheap, high flavour).
5. Rail: tracks beside or across the road, level crossings, a rideable train.
6. Region by region districts (Delhi, then Agra, Jaipur, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad), each with its own landmarks and traffic mix.
7. Festivals and weather seasons by region.

## 8. How Indian roads look, by kind (owner's photos and research; styles built 18 Nov 2026)
Built as road styles (`src/world/roadstyles.js`, try `?road=nh6` or `?road=ghat`): 
- **`nh6`, National Highway, six lanes:** two carriageways of three 3.5 m lanes, a planted grass median about 4.8 m wide between two raised kerbs, white dashed lane lines, solid white edge lines, a 1.5 m paved shoulder, red-soil verge. Traffic keeps left. This is the look of the Golden Quadrilateral and the Mumbai-Pune expressway in the photos. Lane widths and median follow common Indian practice; the exact IRC:SP:84 tables were not found in the search and should be checked.
- **`ghat`, mountain road:** 6.5 m wide, dashed white centre line, white edge lines, a low stone parapet on the outer edge, red-brown soil on the hill side. The photos (Munnar and Nilgiris tea gardens, Western Ghats) show tight hairpins every few hundred metres; our router keeps a 130 m minimum bend radius, so a hill region needs its own routing numbers (`road.minRadius` about 25 to 40, steeper `maxGrade`) before it looks right. Not done.
- **Marking colours:** white for lane and edge lines; yellow is used for restrictions (no overtaking) and sources disagree on the centre line colour (IRC:35 says white generally, yellow for restrictions); not final.
**Built 18 Nov 2026 on `nh6` roads** (`src/world/highway_plan.js`, `highway.js`): W-beam guardrails on both outer edges (solid for the physics), kilometre stones every 1 km (yellow top), green overhead **sign gantries** every 1.5 km with the nearest destinations in Hindi and English and a distance counting down (names from `india_network.destinationsFor`, distances compressed at 1:25), and **toll plazas** every 7 km: yellow canopy, islands and booths between the lanes, a blue "TOLL PLAZA" board; lanes stay clear (the self-test checks it). Cars do not stop or pay yet. The player now also starts in the correct lane for the region's drive side (left in India). Still not built, all visible in the photos: **service roads** beside the highway behind a concrete barrier, **elevated interchanges and cloverleaf flyovers** (the night photo and the aerial of a multi-level interchange), **flyovers and underpasses with waterlogging**, a **railway line running beside the highway**, **truck lay-bys**, and per-state differences: Rajasthan and Gujarat (wide, straight, desert-dust verges, camels and tractors), Punjab and Haryana (flat divided highways, mustard fields), Uttar Pradesh and Bihar (dense roadside settlement, narrow state roads, mixed traffic), Maharashtra ghats (concrete expressway through monsoon green hills, tunnels), Kerala (narrow, built-up, palm-lined), Tamil Nadu and Karnataka (tea and coffee hills, hairpins), Himachal and Ladakh (cliff-side single-carriageway, rock fall barriers).

## 7. Decisions needed from the owner
(1) OSM/ODbL yes or no for real alignments; (2) continuous world or hub-and-legs; (3) scale 1:20 or 1:30 for highways; (4) which cities come first after Mumbai; (5) licensed or original music; (6) which official outline to use for the map.

## 9. Owner decisions (18 Nov 2026) and the recommended design
Decided by the owner: (a) **use real road lines**; (b) highway compression **like GTA**, not a gentle 1:25; (c) **one continuous world**, generated and released by distance so the browser holds little at a time.
Assessment (Claude, not yet reviewed by Anshul): the three decisions fit together if the real data is used as a **skeleton** and the game generates the detail:
1. **Real road lines as the macro path.** At 1:100 a real 100 m bend becomes 1 m, so real polylines cannot be drawn at game scale. Use real alignments only as waypoints (one every real 5 to 10 km, so every 50 to 100 game m) and let the existing procedural router make the local bends and gradients between them. Cities, junctions and which road joins which come from the data; the curves are ours.
2. **Data sources.** Natural Earth roads (public domain, coarse, major roads only; coverage of India to be checked) and NASA SRTM elevation (public domain) can supply the national skeleton with no share-alike duty. OpenStreetMap (ODbL) only for city districts, with credit on screen and the derived road database published under ODbL, separate from game code and art; the owner should confirm that reading with a lawyer. All of it is converted offline by a tool in `tools/` into small tiles shipped with the game; nothing is fetched from the web at run time.
3. **Scale.** Inter-city highways about **1:100** (Delhi to Mumbai about 14 game km, roughly 7 minutes at 120 km/h; Delhi to Kanyakumari about 28 game km, 14 minutes), cities near full scale. Cities are therefore discs of detailed map joined by compressed highways (a graph with real-length-over-100 edges), not one uniform-scale picture. 1:25 would make a coast-to-coast drive an hour or more.
4. **One continuous world.** Sectors (N8) generate from (seed, region data, position) when you approach and are released behind; far terrain is a coarse global heightmap mesh (about 1 MB for the whole country at 1:100). Needs, in order: (i) a road **network** (junctions, many roads) instead of one polyline from the start; (ii) the navigation graph and traffic over that network; (iii) region data attached to positions (which state, which look), blended at borders; (iv) floating-origin rebasing across 30 km (already in place) and physics limited to the loaded radius.
5. **Fast travel.** Trains and a map teleport so no one drives the whole country twice.
Order of work: tile tool and Natural Earth/SRTM import (offline) -> the national skeleton as a network in the game with only highways and a coarse landscape -> junctions and network traffic -> city discs from OSM one at a time (Mumbai first) -> regions blended -> rail. Each step shippable on its own.

**Built 18 Nov 2026 (first step of the order above):** game space and the skeleton. `india_network.js` now has `toGame`/`fromGame` (flat map about the centre of India, 1:100 by default: 1 real km = 10 game metres; India is about 24 x 29 game km), `smoothPath` and `buildSkeleton`; `tools/build_india_skeleton.mjs` writes `assets/india/skeleton.json` (32 KB: 98 cities, 28 roads, 6 railways as smoothed paths in game metres; the self-test fails if the file is out of date). Delhi to Mumbai is about 12.8 game km along the centre path, about 14 with the bends the route planner adds. Real alignments plug in through `data/india_waypoints.json` (not in git) and replace the straight legs. The map page `india_map.html` now defaults to 1:100. Data sources, licences and what needs approval: `docs/OPEN_DATA.md`.

**Built 18 Nov 2026: a real highway you can drive.** `?india=<road id>&from=<city id>&toward=<city id>` (for example `?play&india=nh-delhi-lucknow&from=delhi&toward=lucknow`, road ids are in `src/world/india_network.js` and the map page) loads `assets/india/skeleton.json`, builds a `RoadNetwork` (`src/world/network.js`), takes the path of that highway from the start city towards the other (smoothed by `cleanPath`) and gives it to the road router as `config.road.guide`; the road then follows the guide through procedural land (the region nearest to the start city, six-lane national highway look for the Golden Quadrilateral and the two corridors). Sign gantries name the next cities and the real distance left (game metres x 100); a readout shows the next city; the OpenStreetMap credit is on screen. Tested: the road stays within 450 m of the guide and keeps advancing along it. **Limits:** one road only (no junction yet: at a city the path ends), the land is the region recipe and not the real elevation, cities are just a name on a sign (no city world inserted), traffic and pedestrians are the old single-road systems, and nothing has been measured on a phone.

**Built 18 Nov 2026: the junction network (first increment).** Two ways in: `?india=<road id>&from=<city>&toward=<city>` (one road) and `?india=route&from=<city>&to=<city>` (the shortest way over several roads as one continuous drive: `RoadNetwork.routeGuide`). On the way, a blue **city gate** board (Hindi and English name) stands over the road about 90 m before each city, and within 150 game metres of a city a **junction panel** lists every road you can take there (`RoadNetwork.junctionChoices`: the next city each way, the highway number, the real distance): "keep going" continues, any other choice regenerates the world at that city on that road (a short loading screen; seed, quality and drive mode are kept). Checked in the browser: Delhi to Mumbai shows the Jaipur gate and the panel, and choosing NH 62 loads Jaipur to Jodhpur. **Design note:** the engine still holds one road at a time. The junction is a choice point, not a physical crossing of two roads you can see; building real side roads that coexist (several `Road` objects, traffic and navigation over all of them, seamless streaming) is the next, larger step. The data has gaps (for example Jaipur offers Ajmer twice and no Kota), because only the corridors we entered exist.

**Built 18 Nov 2026: several roads in one world (multi-road engine, first increment).** `src/world/roadset.js` (`RoadSet`) answers the terrain's, ground colour's, scenery clearing's and wheel surface's questions (nearest, grade, drive, style) for every road at once by the nearest road; with one road it behaves exactly as before (self-test compares the land with and without a branch). `world/router.js` roads can now start on another road (`config.road.start` { x, y, z }, heights ease from the start height so the branch meets the road it leaves level) and can be guided on both arms (`guide`, `guideBack`). In `?india=` mode `main.js` generates, near each city of the route, the other highways through that city as real roads that cross the player's road (each follows its own highway path on both sides of the city), graded into the land, with their own meshes, gantries and rails; they are removed when you are more than 3.6 km away. Checked in the browser: at Delhi six roads form a star junction; the world still steps at about 3 ms per headless frame. **Limits:** only the player's road has traffic, pedestrians and the navigation graph; the roads overlap flat at the crossing (no ramps, interchanges, traffic lights or lane paint cut-outs: the paint of one road crosses the other); the physics surface and mesh of a branch exist but its bridges, tunnels and retaining walls are only drawn, not in the collision list; a branch is built once per approach (all roads through a city, including a parallel corridor); the junction panel still regenerates the world rather than letting you simply drive onto the branch (you now can drive onto it, and the panel is a shortcut); nothing is measured on a phone.

**Built 18 Nov 2026: traffic on every road.** `Traffic.addRoad(road)` gives each side road its own `TrafficSim` (cars, lanes, speed limit and the toll slowing all come from that road's style), drawn with the same instanced meshes as the main road (no extra draw calls per road) and added to the physics obstacle circles. A side road only runs while the player is within 700 m of it (off again beyond 1,000 m) and at a lighter level than the main road (`branchMode`: Low or Mid stay Low, High becomes Mid), so the whole world stays within about 25 cars at the top setting. Checked in the browser at Delhi: cars appear on all five crossing roads and on the main road, no console errors. **Limits:** cars never turn from one road onto another and do not give way or stop at the crossing (cars of two roads can drive through each other at the junction; only the player is blocked by cars), no traffic lights at junctions, pedestrians and the navigation graph (N10) are still on the main road only, and a road beyond 1,000 m is empty until you come back (cars respawn, they do not continue).

**Built 19 Nov 2026: crossing behaviour at junctions.** `src/traffic/junction.js` (`Junction`): the roads that cross at a city share one signal: at most three phases (so five or six roads wait at most about 40 s), each green 14 s, amber 3 s, then 2 s all red; never green on two roads at once. The cars of each road's own simulation stop at a line 9 m before the crossing on red (and on amber when they cannot stop), and about one car in five that reaches the centre turns: it is handed to another road's simulation (checks the other road is running, has room and a free lane; a short blend of position and heading hides the jump). `src/world/junction_signals.js` puts a pole with a red, amber and green lamp on the roadside before the crossing for each direction of each road. `main.js` builds the junction when the side roads are built and removes it with them. Checked: the self-test shows cars waiting at the line on red, no car in two simulations, the car cap respected, turns happening; in the browser at Delhi six roads cycle through their phases, cars turned (6 in the first minutes) and the signal poles stand at the crossing. **Limits:** the player is not forced to obey the signal and cars do not avoid a player running a red (they only stop for the red); no yielding between cars inside the crossing, no turn lanes or indicators, a turned car appears in the middle of the other road with a short blend rather than driving the curve, signals only at junctions the player is near (the side roads exist only within 3.6 km), the lamps are small, and nothing is measured on a phone.

**Built 19 Nov 2026: real elevation and land cover.** `tools/fetch_india_terrain.py` downloaded 528 elevation tiles (AWS Terrain Tiles) and 64 land cover blocks (Esri / Impact Observatory 10 m) into `data/raw` (git-ignored); `tools/build_india_grids.py` turned them into `assets/india/terrain.bin` (3.2 MB: 620 x 670 cells of 5 real km, elevation, relief, tree, crop, grass, bare, built-up) with `terrain.json`. In `?india=` mode `terrain.js` uses it (`config.terrain.macro`): the land is the real relief times 0.035 (a 5,000 m pass is 175 game metres; a first try at 0.08 gave knife-edge ridges at 1:100), procedural hills and ridges on top whose strength follows the real relief, sea where the data is below sea level (cities and roads are kept on land by a corridor floor), forest density from the tree cover, farmland patchwork where the land is cropland, ochre bare ground, grey built-up land, and fewer plants on bare and built land. Checked: Delhi 227 m, Bhopal 527 m, Manali 2,442 m, Leh 3,489 m in the grid; the Manali to Leh road climbs to about 5,000 m through snow; Delhi to Jaipur runs over 190 to 410 m semi-arid plain. `?real=off` keeps the procedural land. **Limits:** 5 km cells (no river valleys, small lakes or fine coast), vertical scale is a taste choice, the nine regions and non-India modes still use recipes, no rivers or lakes from data, the Esri land cover year is unconfirmed, and the look of the Himalayan snow and the farmland was judged in one view each. No images of expressways and their surroundings reached me with the request; the land and road look were matched to the earlier photos only.

**Built 20 Nov 2026: Travel.** The pause menu and the title screen have a Travel button: it lists the cities reachable by road from where you are (the nearest city to you in an India drive, or to the place a region world is an impression of), with real distance and the drive time at 1:100 and 100 km/h; picking one loads `?india=route&from=<here>&to=<there>`. From the Kerala world you reach Mumbai this way (Kochi to Mumbai is about 1,193 km, about 7 minutes of driving, over NH 66 and the Konkan coast cities). Any city can also be reached by the junction panel when you are in a drive. Limits: loading screens between legs; the Travel list shows cities, towns are not offered; no map picker (the map page `india_map.html` shows the network); no fast travel by rail.

**Built 20 Nov 2026: one world, and Explore cities.** Play (no address options) is now `?india=world`: a start at a city (`RoadNetwork.worldStart`, default Mumbai or the saved city), then every highway through each city you near is built (`buildCityRoads`: the other highways get their guide from the anchor road's point, junction arms for all roads), distance-loaded and released behind you (`updateBranches`), no reload. The HUD and navigation work on whatever highway you are on; Travel has Navigate (guide to a city) and fast travel (a short reload at the city). The old locations list is the **Explore cities** section (`?loc=<region>`), separate worlds made fresh when picked, with an India map button back. Not built: far-arm gates and signs on branch roads, off-road roaming, rail.
