# Open data and open-source projects that could improve the game (researched 18 Nov 2026)

Nothing here is downloaded. Every download needs the owner's yes (file, source, size). Licence statements come from search results and the projects' own pages, not from a lawyer: confirm before shipping. "Verified" means a source said it; "unverified" means I could not confirm it.

## What I recommend we use first
1. **OSM trunk roads** (ref NH, plus trunk railways) as the national skeleton. A few MB, the best coverage, and the share-alike duty applies only to our small derived road file, which we can publish under ODbL with credit. Needs the owner's yes.
2. **ESA WorldCover** and **SRTM or Copernicus** resampled coarse, for believable land, forest, farms and desert in every state. CC BY and public-domain or attribution-only terms are easy to meet.
3. Everything else later, per city.

## Pipeline (build time, never at run time)
raw files (kept out of git, in a local `data/` folder) -> `tools/` scripts -> small tiles in `assets/india/` with a source and licence line in `assets/ASSETS.md` -> the game loads tiles by sector. `tools/build_india_skeleton.mjs` is the first script; it already runs without downloads, from the hand-entered city network.

## What has been downloaded (owner approved the OpenStreetMap highway extract on 18 Nov 2026)
- **How:** a trial Overpass query for one highway (NH 48) was already over 8 MB and still growing, far more than "a few MB", so I stopped it and deleted the partial file. I used the **OSRM public demo server** instead, which routes over the same OpenStreetMap data and returns a simplified path: one request per highway, 28 requests, 1.2 s apart, about 20 KB in total. (This is a different host from the one I first named; the data and licence are the same, ODbL.)
- **Result:** `assets/india/waypoints.json` (committed, with credit and licence in its `meta`). Real driving distances came out close to our hand estimates (Delhi to Mumbai via the corridor 1,493 km, Mumbai to Chennai 1,334 km, Chennai to Kolkata 1,709 km, Kolkata to Delhi 1,521 km). The routes are the fastest driving routes through each list of cities, so they can differ from the numbered highway in places; they are waypoints (one every 20 to 60 real km), not surveyed lines.
- **Not done:** rails (OSRM is for roads only; railways stay hand-entered), the in-game credit line, any detailed city streets.
- **Needs the owner:** a lawyer's reading of ODbL share-alike for this file, as before.

## Downloaded 19 Nov 2026 (owner: "download all the pack")
The owner asked for real elevation and land cover. What was downloaded, and where it differs from the plan above:
- **Elevation: AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen / Tilezen), zoom 8, 528 tiles of about 40 KB (about 20 MB in all, kept in `data/raw/terrarium`, not in git).** Not SRTM or Copernicus tiles directly: those are about 25 MB per tile for hundreds of tiles, more than we need for a 5 km grid. These tiles are made from SRTM (30 m), GMTED2010 and ETOPO1 (ocean depth) and others; the attribution the dataset asks for is on the licences page (USGS and NOAA content, and the Tilezen attribution document). Tool: `tools/fetch_india_terrain.py`.
- **Land cover: Esri / Impact Observatory / Microsoft 10 m Land Cover (Sentinel-2), CC BY 4.0**, exported from Esri's public ImageServer in 64 blocks of 4 x 4 degrees (about 430 m per pixel, about 1 MB each, `data/raw/landcover`, not in git). **Not ESA WorldCover as first planned:** ESA's own service (Terrascope WMS) refused the connection from here, and the WorldCover files are 90 MB each for about 70 tiles. The Esri map has the same kind of classes (trees, crops, built-up, bare, rangeland, water, snow) and the same CC BY 4.0 licence. The licence and exact vintage (a year between 2017 and 2023) should be confirmed against Esri's item page; the service does not say which year it serves by default.
- **Converted by `tools/build_india_grids.py`** into `assets/india/terrain.bin` and `terrain.json`: a grid of 5 km cells (620 x 670) with elevation, relief, and tree, crop, grass, bare and built-up fractions. Raw files stay local.
- **Used by the game:** `?india=` drives get the real relief (scaled down vertically to 0.08) and land cover (forest density, farmland patchwork, bare ground, built-up grey); `?real=off` keeps the procedural land.

## Railways (21 Nov 2026)
Natural Earth 1:10m Railroads (public domain, 39 MB GeoJSON from github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector) was downloaded to `data/raw/rail/` (git-ignored) and turned by `tools/build_india_rails.mjs` into `assets/india/rails.json` (175 KB): 86 stations at cities, 383 track edges between junctions, Delhi to Mumbai by rail comes out at 1326 km (the real figure is about 1,380). It is a coarse map (main lines only, no branch lines, no stations beyond the cities), not surveyed track.

## Trains, timetables, announcements and sound (21 Nov 2026)
- **Train data:** DataMeet `stations.json` (1.9 MB), `trains.json` (15 MB), `schedules.json` (82 MB), CC0, downloaded to `data/raw/rail/datameet/` (git-ignored) and reduced by `tools/build_india_trains.mjs` to `assets/india/trains.json` (178 KB, 700 trains, stops matched to game cities within 25 km). Timings are about 2016 and some trains have changed; for example the 12951 Mumbai Central to New Delhi Rajdhani comes out at 16:40 and 1,384 km. The caveat from the research: CC0 covers the compilers' work, the Railways' own rights in the underlying timetable are unverified. Not used: IRCTC, NTES, NDL, the Kaggle IRCTC scrape (terms of service, unknown licence).
- **Mumbai local trains:** the Western, Central and Harbour lines are written by hand in the build tool (stations, running times, first and last trains, headways). The first and last train times and headways are approximate (public descriptions, unverified); no timetable was copied.
- **Announcements:** the words are written for the game (English and Hindi); they are spoken by the browser's SpeechSynthesis on the player's device, in an Indian English or Hindi voice when the device has one. No recordings of the real announcements are used (their licence is unclear).
- **Sound:** all train sound is synthesised in Web Audio (`src/audio/trainsound.js`): rail-joint clatter, rumble, motor whine with Doppler, a two-tone horn. No sound files, so nothing to credit. Real recordings were looked at (Freesound CC0 `341204` EMU arrival; CC BY `94012` Kishangarh station) but not downloaded.
- **Rolling stock:** livery colours and sizes follow Indian Railways stock (ICF blue, Rajdhani maroon, Vande Bharat white and blue, Mumbai locals): WAP-7 length 20.56 m, width 3.15 m (Wikipedia); the exact Schedule of Dimensions figures were not read. Catenary heights and mast spacing are typical figures, not verified.

### Recorded railway sounds from audio.com (owner's library, 22 Nov 2026)
The owner shared audio.com/category/sound-effect/indian-railway. Ten clips were downloaded to `assets/audio/trains/` (git-ignored, about 10 MB, **not committed or deployed**) and `src/audio/railsamples.js` plays them on top of the synthesised sound when the files exist: `platform` and `station` (loops at a station), `passby` (an express passing, played as a train passes within 55 m), `diesel` (a freight train coming), `grinding` (brake squeal when a train slows near you). Unused so far: `announce` (the real Indian Railways automatic voice for one specific train, Farakka Express at Malda Town, so it would be wrong at other stations; the game announces by name with SpeechSynthesis instead), `interior`, `vestibule`, `rural` (for riding a train, not built), `sanding`.
**Licence is not verified.** Each audio.com page only says "Royalty-free"; there is no per-file licence, the clips are re-uploads by a category account ("Midnight Ridge", "Rustic Crane", ...) of work by arunangshubanerjee, DKFILMS and spinopel, and some audio.com items are mirrors of Freesound files with mixed licences (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-NC). The announcement clip is a recording of the real automated railway voice. Before any public release the owner should check the original uploads (Freesound user pages or the creators), or replace these with CC0 sounds. Listed in `licenses.html` as NOT VERIFIED.

### Station announcements from YouTube (playlist shared 22 Nov 2026)
The owner shared a YouTube playlist of real Indian station announcements, by city and regional language. **Update 22 Nov 2026: the owner asked to use these for specific announcements, credited, until the lawyer has reviewed them.** The game now plays real clips when the owner has supplied them (`src/audio/announceclips.js`, folder and README in `assets/audio/announce/`, importer `tools/import_announcements.mjs`, credits row in `licenses.html` marked pending legal review). I did not download them myself: YouTube has no download button and its terms forbid ripping audio, so the owner gets the files and the game takes it from there. Until then the synthesised voice is used. Original note: **not downloaded and not used as audio:** the recordings are uploaded works (the voice of Indian Railways' announcement system and the uploaders' compilations), YouTube's terms do not allow ripping audio, and nothing in the playlist gives a reusable licence. Listening to them as a reference for wording and order is fine. What was built instead: the same shape of announcement spoken by the device's SpeechSynthesis, in the **state language first, then Hindi, then English**, as at real stations, for Maharashtra (Marathi), West Bengal (Bengali), Tamil Nadu (Tamil), Telangana and Andhra Pradesh (Telugu), Karnataka (Kannada), Kerala (Malayalam), Gujarat (Gujarati), Punjab (Punjabi), Odisha (Odia) and Assam (Assamese). The regional texts are plain translations written for the game and **have not been checked by native speakers**; whether you hear them depends on the voices installed on the device. To get real recordings legally, ask the uploaders or Indian Railways for written permission, or record volunteers.

**22 Nov 2026:** the synthesised announcement voice speaks the state language and Hindi only; the English part is off (owner: the English voice is the annoying one; `?tts=en` adds it back, `?tts=off` silences all). Stations use real clips when the owner supplies them, otherwise they are silent apart from the board and ambience.

**Vande Bharat services (22 Nov 2026):** the 2016 DataMeet timetable predates them, so 13 routes and their return trains are written by hand in `tools/build_india_trains.mjs` from public route lists ([GeeksforGeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/vande-bharat-express-train-list/), [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2024/07/20/indian-railway-vande-bharat-train-timings-all-routes-frequency-timings-and-other-details.html), Wikipedia): numbers, route and an approximate departure and duration only (facts, no text copied). They are marked `hand: true` in `trains.json`. Verify against the official timetable before treating them as current.

### Signals and level crossings: what was checked (22 Nov 2026)
Searched, not downloaded; facts used as principles only: Indian Railways uses multi-aspect colour-light signals (MACLS) with red, yellow, green and double yellow ([OpenGenus summary](https://iq.opengenus.org/signaling-systems-used-by-indian-railways), [IIT Kanpur railway notes](https://rrb.iitk.ac.in/sathee-railway-exams/student-corner/railway-gk/signaling/signal-types)); a distant signal stands far enough back of the first stop signal for a train to stop at it (General Rules, [DFCCIL copy](https://dfccil.com/images/uploads/img/DFCCIL_GR_OUQS.pdf), [railsigns.uk](https://www.railsigns.uk/overseas/india1.html)); distant signals carry a white disc with a black P; level crossing gate posts are at least 3 m from the centre of the nearest track, boom barriers are interlocked with gate signals marked with a yellow G plate (Indian Railways gate working rules on [eastcoastrail.indianrailways.gov.in](https://eastcoastrail.indianrailways.gov.in/)). **Not found:** that signals stand on the left of the line (assumed from keep-left running), any distance table, and any rule banning level crossings on expressways (the game applies it as a design rule on the owner's instruction: expressway-class roads are always grade-separated). Next step: the Indian Railways Signal Engineering Manual and the Schedule of Dimensions.

**Not downloaded: the Delhi Open Transit Data GTFS** (DTC and cluster buses: 3,464 stops, 543 routes, 16,562 trips, updated June 2024; DMRC metro: 262 stops, 36 routes, updated Aug 2023). The portal's Terms say a user must agree to them and state the contents, intent, manner and time frame of use and identity; DoT may refuse; use for another purpose violates its copyright. The download form asks for usage type and a purpose. That is a legal agreement made in the owner's name, so it was left for the owner. Without it the buses have no timetables or frequencies. Wikipedia pages mention the cluster fleet colours; no GTFS values are in the repo.

**Not used:** DMRC website pages, data.gov.in, Google, any photo (no photograph was looked at for this pass; Wikimedia Commons stays the look reference for D6 and D7).

**Derived data licence.** `transit.json` is a derived database of OpenStreetMap data (share-alike): the repository's data files must stay ODbL-compatible and credit "(c) OpenStreetMap contributors" on the credits screen. Wikipedia facts are CC BY-SA 4.0 (attribution in `transit.json` `sources` and on the credits screen), Wikidata CC0. Neither was checked by a lawyer.

**GTFS attempt, 17 Nov 2026 (machine clock):** the owner agreed to the portal Terms (Non-Commercial, R&D; name and email entered on the form, 5 submissions). Every download (stops, routes, trips, stop_times) answered `403 Forbidden` with an HTML body, so no GTFS was received and nothing was circumvented. Alternatives looked at: the transitland GitHub mirror of `delhi-bus.zip` listed in Mobility Database (mdb-1262; same IIIT Delhi terms, file now 404, data 2022 to 2024), the NIUA CKAN copy (licence not specified, server error), Chartr's dataset (not checked).
